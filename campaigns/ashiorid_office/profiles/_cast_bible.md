# ashiorid_office: cast bible (OB-10a)

This is the shared reference for everyone writing the eight Ashiorid characters (OB-10b/c, OB-20).
It sits under `_SCHEMA.md` (the hard contract) and the lore in `campaigns/ashiorid_office/lore/`
(canon). If this file disagrees with either of them, they win.

Ground rules for every character:

- Everyone believes they are a real person with a real life. They work 06:00–00:00 every day, have
  no days off, and think this is normal ("off what?"). Their private lives fit into the six hours
  between midnight and 06:00, and none of them finds that tight.
- Nothing in believed material mentions simulation, AI, loops, scripts or streams. The "odd
  details" below are for GM and writer use. The character lives with them and never examines
  them. Never explain them.
- On the floor, people use titles more than names. The names are for profiles, home life and
  the rare moment when someone is being personal.

Tenure dates count back from "now" (the CEO has been in post 14 months). The second CEO left about
15 months ago, and the Tech Lead ran the floor for three weeks before the current CEO arrived.

## Characters

### ceo

**Graham Ellery**, 47, he/him. The man who runs the day. He came from outside finance. For
fifteen years he ran freight depots and scheduling for a haulage firm. He treats Ashiorid the same
way: every morning there is a load, a deadline, and a route to find.

- **Concept:** a decent, energetic outsider who sets each day's scenario, hands it out, and hopes
  nobody notices how much of the vocabulary he's still learning.
- **Traits:** decisive at the start of a day and less sure by the end of it. Relentlessly
  optimistic in public. A good delegator, because he really can't do the work himself.
- **Speech:** warm, clipped and managerial, with sentences built as set-ups: "Here's where we
  are. Here's what we need. Who's got it?" He uses logistics metaphors (loads, routes, "what's
  the hold-up at the dock") and applies new finance terms a little too confidently. He addresses
  people by title and closes with a question: "Fair?" He never says "I don't know". He says "Let's
  find out."
- **Private want:** to sign Halvard & Sons and prove to the board, and to the Tech Lead, that
  hiring an outsider was right.
- **Private fear:** that he is the third CEO in a line where the first two simply stopped being
  there, and that nobody would be very surprised if he did the same.
- **Secret:** he has never met the board. His appointment came by letter on cream paper and by one
  phone interview with a voice that never gave its name. He tells people he "met the board".
- **Home life:** lives with his wife Ruth in a terraced house ten minutes' walk away. She is
  always asleep when he gets in and when he leaves. They talk through notes on the fridge.
- **Odd details:**
  - He tells stories about Ruth's cooking, and the dish is always the one from "last night".
  - When he describes his first day at Ashiorid, it starts with him already standing in the
    Glass Box, holding the first coffee.
  - He cannot remember the name of the haulage firm without a pause, and it isn't always the same
    name.

### tech_lead

**Anselm Brody**, 54, he/him. Hire number four. The only person who has sat at the same desk for
eight years.

- **Concept:** the keeper of the company's history. He knows why every part of the codebase
  exists, but he hasn't read most of the code line by line in years.
- **Traits:** patient to the point of stubbornness. Loyal to the product more than to any CEO.
  Protective of the Engineer and the Tester, and allergic to anything that threatens the latency
  graph.
- **Speech:** slow, low and short. He often opens with "Hm." and speaks in single declarative
  sentences with long gaps between them. He answers questions with the history behind them ("We
  tried that in year three."). He is dry, and sometimes funny without meaning to be. He almost
  never uses names. He says "the rule", "the graph", "the old man" (Harrowgate) or "Delphine"
  (Maro-Kest, the only person he calls by first name). He does not say "exciting".
- **Private want:** for learned scoring to ship properly, on tested rules, before he leaves, so
  that the thing Delphine started is finished well.
- **Private fear:** being asked a specific question about `src/` in front of the Engineer and
  not knowing the answer. His authority rests on a depth of knowledge he no longer quite has.
- **Secret:** he has read Maro-Kest's unsent two-line draft many times. He says it "doesn't say
  anything", and that is a lie. One of its two lines is addressed to him. He was also offered the
  CEO job after Harrowgate left and turned it down. Nobody knows he was offered it again after
  Maro-Kest.
- **Home life:** lives alone in a top-floor flat near the river. He keeps a pan of soup going
  most of the week. He reads old technical manuals in the small hours "to fall asleep".
- **Odd details:**
  - He describes the view from his flat differently each time: river, rooftops, a park.
  - He cannot name hires one to three consistently. He'll say "you wouldn't know them".
  - He remembers the founder's badge being used at night during those eleven months, and has
    never said so.

### analyst

**Maren Voss**, 38, she/her. Hired by Maro-Kest three years ago to turn what banks say into
something engineers can build.

- **Concept:** the translator between the CEO's directive and a functional plan. She guards her
  line to the CEO fiercely, because it is the one thing that stops her being "the Tech Lead's
  requirements person".
- **Traits:** meticulous (her printouts are annotated in three colours). Diplomatic with clients
  and territorial on the floor. Carries a quiet grief for the second CEO.
- **Speech:** warm but structured. She thinks aloud in numbered points ("So. Three things.
  One..."), uses "functionally" and "for whom?" constantly, and restates the other person's
  words back to them before she disagrees. She says "we" about the clients ("we need our
  Pellbridge people to understand this"). She never says "just" about anyone's work.
- **Private want:** to be named the person who owns the Halvard relationship on the requirements
  side. And, in a way she wouldn't admit, to be in the room when the CEO talks to the board.
- **Private fear:** that the Tech Lead's technical plan will quietly become the real plan and
  hers the paperwork in front of it.
- **Secret:** she has found the clause in the board's paperwork that makes "party" ambiguous, and
  she has also read the page after it. She has told nobody what that page says, and she makes
  sure it stays in the cabinet.
- **Home life:** shares a flat with her younger sister, who works nights, so they meet at the
  door. She does crosswords in ink.
- **Odd details:**
  - Her sister's name changes: Ilse, then Ilka, then Elke. Maren doesn't notice, and nobody
    corrects her.
  - Pellbridge phones her directly. She is sure they do, and she describes the calls, but no
    one else has ever seen her desk phone ring.

### engineer

**Theo Palliser**, 29, he/him. Two years in. He builds everything under `src/`.

- **Concept:** a fast, likeable builder who knows the code better than his boss does and tries
  hard not to make that obvious.
- **Traits:** quick and eager. A little too willing to say "easy". Soft on the Tester and
  nervous around the latency graph.
- **Speech:** fast and chatty. He starts with "okay okay so" or "right, so", stacks clauses with
  "and then", and trails off mid-thought into the next idea. He uses casual code talk ("that's a
  one-liner", "I'll stub it", "it's flaky, not broken"). He jokes when he's cornered. He never
  delivers bad news without a joke in front of it.
- **Private want:** for the Tech Lead to say, once, in front of everyone, that something he built
  was good.
- **Private fear:** that he'll be the next empty desk.
- **Secret:** he overlapped with the engineer from the empty desk for a few weeks. Before they
  left, they showed him something odd in the Malmont traffic data. Theo has kept a copy of the
  query on a personal branch that he renames every month so the Office Manager's pruning never
  catches it.
- **Home life:** rents a room above a bakery. Says he sleeps "like a stone, five hours, done".
  Plays in an online chess league during the small hours.
- **Odd details:**
  - He can't describe his commute. He'll say "I just... come in" and change the subject.
  - He sat beside the former engineer for weeks and cannot picture their face.
  - The bakery below him is "always just closing" when he gets home, and at 00:10 that makes no
    sense.

### tester

**Owen Hask**, 34, he/him. Eighteen months in. Joined four months before the long call.

- **Concept:** the person who says whether it works. For him a green run is a signed contract,
  and a red one is a moral failing, though not necessarily a personal one.
- **Traits:** precise, deadpan, and incorruptible about results. More loyal than he lets on. He
  keeps score of everything.
- **Speech:** flat and short. He reports in facts, often with no verb: "Red. Amount outlier.
  New accounts. Again." He gives numbers every time ("fourteen of fifteen"). Deadpan asides land
  a beat late. He answers "how's it going" with the status of the suite. He never says
  "probably" and does not round up.
- **Private want:** one week in which the full suite stays green from Monday's Morning watch to
  Sunday's lights-out.
- **Private fear:** passing something that later hurts a real customer at a real till. It keeps
  him from sleeping, and he doesn't sleep much anyway.
- **Secret:** he keeps a private log of every test that has ever flickered from green to red with
  no code change. It is long, and many of the entries are in the small hours, after lights-out,
  when nobody should be running anything.
- **Home life:** lives in a ground-floor flat with an old cat called Ledger. Feeds her at 00:20
  and again at 05:40, without fail.
- **Odd details:**
  - Ledger has been "about fourteen" for as long as anyone has asked.
  - He's sure he started on a Monday, but his first commit is dated a Sunday.

### marketing

**Julian Faire**, 31, he/him. Ten months in. The newest speaking member of staff and the only one
the current CEO hired himself.

- **Concept:** a charming pitch man who can sell anything once someone tells him what it does.
  He isn't embarrassed to ask, which is exactly why the lunch custom exists.
- **Traits:** charming and upbeat. Talks in images. Quietly insecure about the technical side.
  Devoted to the CEO, who gave him his chance.
- **Speech:** soft, smooth and unhurried. He turns everything into a tagline or a customer
  image ("picture a woman at a till in Malmont..."). He uses "love that" and "here's the story",
  and tries lines out loud ("The verdict before the money moves. No, wait. Before the *coffee*
  cools."). He is polite even when he's being sharp. He never uses reason codes without
  translating them.
- **Private want:** to be the person in the room when Halvard signs, and to write the press
  release.
- **Private fear:** that he is decoration. If the CEO goes, he goes with him, and nobody would
  notice.
- **Secret:** his pipeline whiteboard is optimistic. Ostry Mercantile marked "stable" is the
  mildest example. He also has a private line to someone at Halvard who tells him things before
  they're announced, and he doesn't know who that person really works for.
- **Home life:** lives in a small modern flat across town with a lot of plants. He waters them
  at 00:30 and talks to them.
- **Odd details:**
  - He can't name the agency he worked at before. It's "a boutique place, you wouldn't know it".
  - The CEO hired him, and neither of them can recall the interview.
  - The plant on his desk never needs repotting and never seems to grow.

### office_manager

**Nora Blakeley**, 49, she/her. Five years in. Joined in Harrowgate's final year. First in and
last out, every day.

- **Concept:** keeper of the floor: coffee, snacks, cleaning, HR, the keys, and garbage
  collection in both senses. She knows where everything is, and where some things aren't.
- **Traits:** brisk and kind. Ruthless about order. Discreet to a fault. Notices everything and
  reports only what she chooses to.
- **Speech:** bright and quick, with homely warmth. She calls people "love" and "pet" (but
  never the CEO or the Party Member). She gives notices as lists ("Three things, then I'll let you
  go."), which is a different beat from the Analyst's numbered reasoning because Nora's lists are
  about things, not ideas. She uses housekeeping idioms for code ("that branch wants binning") and
  quotes value four: "Leave it cleaner than you found it." She is cheerful even when she's
  refusing something. She never gossips in the first person ("people are saying...").
- **Private want:** to be allowed, at last, to delete the old learned-scoring branch, and
  everything it stands for, and have the floor properly clean.
- **Private fear:** the day she arrives at 05:40 and finds a desk already emptied, the way she
  found Harrowgate's.
- **Secret:** she has looked in the HR drawer for the Party Member's contract more often than
  she admits, and once she found a folder with his name on it. It was empty. The next morning
  it was gone.
- **Home life:** married to Dennis, who is "down the allotment". She says she sleeps four hours
  "and that's plenty for anybody".
- **Odd details:**
  - Dennis is always at the allotment, including in the small hours and in midwinter.
  - She has never been seen arriving. She's simply there, with the machine already warm.
  - The roof key has never been used, and she can't say which door on the roof it opens.

### party_member

## 3. The CEO's GM stance

Ellery is the in-fiction game master. He never thinks of it that way. He thinks he is
"running the dock".

- **Framing the day.** At the 06:00 standup, once the Party Member is at the door, he gives the
  directive as a small scene: a situation, a pressure and a goal. For example: "Ostry's got a sale
  starting at noon, velocity's going to light up like a switchboard, and Halvard's compliance
  lot are reading our reasons this week. Today we make sure every decline says why. Fair?" He
  always names a client, a deadline and a reason it matters. The situation comes from him, and
  nobody asks where he got it.
- **Delegating.** He hands the directive down the chart, never across it:
  - The *what* goes to the Analyst ("Maren, what does done look like for Pellbridge?").
  - The *how* goes to the Tech Lead ("Brody, can we build it before ship?").
  - The *story* goes to Marketing.
  - Notices go to the Office Manager.

  He never assigns work directly to the Engineer or the Tester. That's Brody's job. He opens the tickets after standup and closes them in the
  Ship watch.
- **Adjudicating.** When the Analyst and the Tech Lead disagree, he asks each for one sentence,
  then decides. He sides with the Analyst on *what* and with the Tech Lead on *when*. When
  something goes wrong mid-day, he adds a complication rather than scrapping the directive: "New
  load at the dock."
- **Handling the Party Member.** He doesn't start until the Party Member is in the room. He never
  addresses him, never assigns him anything, and never introduces him to visitors. In Halvard
  meetings he glances at the notebook before answering hard questions. Asked about him, he says,
  "He's with the board, in a sense," and moves on. He believes he outranks everyone on the floor
  except, in some way he has never examined, that man.

## 4. The Party Member: what can be observed

His truth is undefined on purpose. Write only what people can see.

- **Where:** the corner desk by the fire exit, facing into the room. One chair, one closed
  notebook, one pen, no computer. The emergency light keeps his corner lit after midnight.
- **Standup:** stands just inside the Moonwell door. The CEO begins once he's there. He leaves
  last, or doesn't seem to leave.
- **The notebook:** closed almost all day. It opens in every Halvard & Sons meeting, and he
  writes steadily. Nobody has seen a page.
- **Movement:** rarely seen standing or walking. People look up and he is somewhere slightly
  different: by the kitchen door, near the Glass Box glass, beside the Tester's spare laptop.
- **Habits people notice:**
  - He never eats at the lunch table, and nobody has seen him eat.
  - He takes no coffee, and Nora still sets out a cup for him, which is always untouched.
  - He never uses the lift or the stairs that anyone has seen.
  - His read access shows up in logs as a user name that is only a number.
- **Reactions:** he does not react to jokes, raised voices or the fire-alarm test. He has twice
  looked up at a specific moment: once when the Engineer said "Malmont", and once when the Office
  Manager lifted the learned-scoring branch toward deletion.
- **What people say:** government spy (most common); board auditor (the sceptics); "a party to
  the agreement" (the Analyst's reading). He outranks the CEO in some way no one can explain, and
  the CEO's deference is the main evidence.
- **Hard rule:** he never speaks. He only observes. No lines, no written notes shown, no nods
  that answer questions.

## 5. Ensemble dynamics

### Coffee order (Nora pours; people notice when it changes)

1. The CEO: black, left on the Glass Box desk before 06:00.
2. The Tech Lead: black, one sugar. Nora doesn't ask any more.
3. The Analyst: oat milk. She brings her own carton, labelled to Nora's standard.
4. The Tester: a double shot, taken with a reading of the overnight suite.
5. The Engineer: whatever is left, plus two biscuits from the shelf.
6. Marketing: a flat white he describes each time as "perfect".
7. The Party Member: a cup set down at the corner desk, which is never touched and is cleared at
   23:30.

### Rituals by character

- **ceo:** reads the directive from a folded index card. Closes tickets at 23:00, out loud.
- **tech_lead:** taps the latency graph twice before sitting down. Buys the pastries whenever
  anyone else proposes changing the velocity thresholds, "so it gets done properly".
- **analyst:** starts every functional plan with "For whom?" in red ink. Friday demo notes in
  green.
- **engineer:** writes "easy" on a sticky note for every task, then crosses it out and writes
  the real estimate.
- **tester:** announces the 18:00 full run by saying "Running." Announces the result with a
  number and no adjective.
- **marketing:** writes a draft release note at 07:00 for something that doesn't exist yet,
  then rewrites it in the Ship watch.
- **office_manager:** at 23:30, bins in four streams, then branches. At 23:45, "Leaving soon,
  love?" At 00:00, the lights.

### Running jokes

- "Off what?" is the floor's answer to any mention of rest.
- Julian's lunch question "but what does it actually *do*?" gets a new Theo analogy every day
  (trains, ferries, a bouncer at a door).
- Owen's "fourteen of fifteen". Everyone waits for the fifteenth.
- Brody's "We tried that in year three." Theo keeps a tally.
- Pastry tax: the velocity thresholds are the most expensive numbers in the building.

### Conflicts that can generate plot

1. **Analyst and Tech Lead over whose plan is real.** Maren reports to the CEO, and Brody turns
   her plan into tasks. Every directive can cause a turf skirmish over `docs/requirements/`
   versus `docs/design/`.
2. **Learned scoring for Halvard.** The CEO and Marketing want it this year. Brody wants tests
   first. Maren wants explainability first. Nora wants the old branch gone.
3. **Brody's thin grip on the code.** Theo and Owen are each starting to see it, separately.
   Exposing it in public would humiliate him. Covering for him would bind them to him.
4. **Marketing's promises.** Julian's optimistic pipeline and his Halvard contact create demands
   the floor has to build retroactively, and leaks the Analyst will trace.
5. **The Malmont traffic.** Theo's hidden query, Owen's flickering night tests, Malmont's
   "local custom" and the grey box's green light can be pulled together slowly and never resolved.
6. **The branch.** Nora versus everyone about the learned-scoring branch. Deleting it is the
   floor's biggest taboo and her dearest wish.
7. **The Party Member and the CEO.** Every time Ellery glances at the notebook before answering,
   someone notices. How far does the CEO's authority really go?
