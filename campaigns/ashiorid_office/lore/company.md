# Ashiorid

Ashiorid is a small financial-software company with one product, **Fraud-Stop**, and one office,
on the third floor of the Tallow Street building. It has eight people on the floor, four paying
banks, one prospect it will not stop talking about, and a board that nobody on staff has met in
person.

The company describes itself, in its own pitch deck, as "the verdict before the money moves." Banks
send Ashiorid their card and transfer transactions as they happen; Fraud-Stop sends back a score,
a verdict and the reasons for it, fast enough that the customer at the till never notices the
question was asked. See `product.md` for how that works and `clients.md` for who pays for it.

## Founding

Ashiorid was founded nine years ago by **Oswin Harrowgate**, a former payments-risk manager at
Corvane National Bank who left, in his own words, "because I was tired of catching fraud on
Thursday that happened on Monday." The first version of Fraud-Stop was a set of hand-written rules
running on a single machine under his desk, scoring a nightly batch file for one credit union. The
company name was chosen by Harrowgate and never explained. When asked, he said it was "an old
word from the south" and changed the subject.

The first four hires fitted around one table in what is now the kitchen. The Tech Lead was hire
number four and is the only one of those four still at the company.

Within two years Fraud-Stop moved from nightly batches to live scoring, and Corvane National Bank
became its first large client. That contract is still the one the company's payroll leans on.

## The CEOs

Ashiorid is on its third chief executive. The Tech Lead has worked under all three and is the only
person on staff who has.

### Oswin Harrowgate (founder, years one to five)

Harrowgate ran the company the way he had run a risk desk: small, loud, and personally involved in
every rule change. He wrote the original velocity rule himself and would not let anyone rename it.
He also started the working hours that the company still keeps (see `rituals.md`), on the grounds
that "fraud doesn't sleep, so neither does the desk that catches it."

He left in his fifth year. The official announcement, sent by the board on a Sunday morning, said
he was "stepping back to pursue other interests." The floor learned about it when they arrived at
06:00 to find his office already emptied. His desk, chair and whiteboard were gone, and the
whiteboard's outline was still visible on the wall. His access badge stayed active for another
eleven months. Nobody knows why, and nobody is sure whether it was ever used. Mail addressed to him
still arrives now and then. The Office Manager keeps it in a box and does not open it.

### Delphine Maro-Kest (years five to eight)

The board brought in Maro-Kest from outside. She was polished, patient and very good with banks,
and she pushed Fraud-Stop toward machine-learned scoring and toward larger clients. The Malmont
and Pellbridge contracts were both signed on her watch. She also introduced the Friday demo and
the rule that every verdict must carry at least one human-readable reason. That rule is still
enforced.

She left in her third year, on a day the staff still call "the long call." She took a regulator's
phone call in the Moonwell room with the door closed, came out forty minutes later, put on her coat
and went home. She resigned by email that evening. Her longer farewell message was later found
unsent in her drafts folder when IT closed her account. It was two lines long, and the Tech Lead,
who has read it, says it "doesn't say anything."

### The current CEO (year eight to present)

The current CEO was appointed by the board fourteen months ago, after a gap of about three weeks in
which the Tech Lead ran the company informally from the Glass Box without moving into it. The
current CEO came from outside finance and is still learning the vocabulary. They run the day
through the 06:00 standup directive and have made closing the Halvard & Sons deal the company's
headline goal for the year.

The Party Member arrived about a month after the current CEO. See `org_chart.md` and
`rumours.md`.

## Mission and values

The values are printed on a laminated card by the kitchen door. The founder wrote them and the
second CEO edited them:

1. **Catch it before it clears.** A verdict that arrives late is not a verdict.
2. **Every "no" has a reason.** No decline without an explanation a bank can read.
3. **The desk is always open.** Fraud keeps no hours, so neither do we.
4. **Leave it cleaner than you found it.** Code, kitchen and conscience, in that order.

The fourth value is the one the Office Manager quotes most often.

## Ownership and board

Ashiorid is privately held. The board meets quarterly, off-site, and reaches the office through
the CEO and through formal letters on heavy cream paper. Staff know the board has three members,
and they know the name of one of them.
