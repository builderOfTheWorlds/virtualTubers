# Rumours

These are the stories people on the floor tell each other at lunch, in the Small Room, or on the
stairs. None of them has been confirmed. Most people believe at least one of them.

## The Party Member is a government spy

This is the rumour everyone knows. The Party Member arrived about a month after the current CEO.
They have no contract in the HR drawer, no computer and no email address, but they have read access
to every system Ashiorid runs. Fraud-Stop scores most of the card traffic for four banks, and
the reasoning on the floor is that any government would want someone sitting next to that.

Believers point out that:

- the Party Member's desk is the only one with a view of every screen and of the Glass Box
- the CEO starts standup only after the Party Member has come in
- the Party Member writes in the closed notebook during meetings about Halvard & Sons, and at no
  other time
- the Office Manager has never seen the Party Member arrive or leave

Sceptics say the Party Member is an auditor the board sent in, or a consultant nobody wanted to
introduce. Nobody has asked the Party Member, since nobody expects an answer.

The word "party" gets argued about most. Some think it means a political party. Others think it's
the legal sense, as in "a party to the agreement." The Analyst, who has read every contract
Ashiorid holds, says there is a clause in the board's paperwork that could be read either way.

## The founder never left

Oswin Harrowgate's badge stayed active for eleven months after he cleared out his office. Some
say the logs showed it being used at night during that time. Others say the logs from that period
were lost when the old server was replaced, which is a rumour in itself. The mail still comes for
him. One version of the story says he still owns a piece of the company through someone else. A
stranger version says he went back to "the south" he used to mention, and that the company's name
comes from there.

## The long call

Nobody knows who was on the other end of the phone in the Moonwell room on the second CEO's last
day. The official account says it was a regulator. The Engineer thinks it was the board. Marketing
has heard that it was Corvane National Bank, threatening to leave. The Tech Lead has read the
unsent draft and won't talk about it, so everyone assumes the Tech Lead knows more than they're
saying.

## The grey box

The server closet holds one unlabelled grey box with a single green light, plugged into the
network with no documentation. The Tech Lead says it's "legacy." The Office Manager says it
was there before she was. The most popular theory is that it's the founder's original machine,
still scoring something for someone. Its power draw appears on the building bill every month.

## The empty desk

The engineer who used to sit at the empty sixth desk left during the second CEO's time. Their
name is still on the old learned-scoring branch, the one the Office Manager has twice been told
not to delete. Staff agree that the engineer left suddenly, and disagree about everything else.
There are three versions of the story:

- They were hired away by Halvard & Sons.
- They found something in the transaction data that they shouldn't have.
- They simply stopped coming in one Monday, and nobody ever followed up.

## The Tech Lead could have been CEO

For the three weeks between the second CEO and the current one, the Tech Lead ran Ashiorid in
practice without once sitting in the Glass Box. The board then appointed an outsider. Some say
the Tech Lead was offered the job twice, once after each departure, and turned it down both times.
Others say the offer never came. The Tech Lead's view on the matter is that the latency graph
"doesn't care who is CEO."

## A smaller one

The Office Manager's key ring has one key that fits no door on the floor. She says it's for the
roof.
