# The Scuttle

**The Scuttle** is a piracy and file-sharing site, the largest and loudest in its corner of the
internet, and the Cyber Police's single oldest open case. It has outlived three of the unit's
captains, two changes of law the department swore would finally shut it down, and every raid the
unit has ever mounted against it.

## What it is

The Scuttle indexes and trackers pirated software, film, music and games, dressed up in a
nautical theme its operators clearly find hilarious: uploads are "cargo," the most popular
torrents are "flagship hauls," moderators are "quartermasters," and the site's front page carries
a rotating banner reading "NO QUARTER, NO ADS." It makes money nobody can fully trace, through
ad networks that dissolve and reform under new names faster than the unit can subpoena them.

## The crew that runs it

The people who run The Scuttle call themselves **privateers**, in on the joke the same way the
site's whole aesthetic is in on the joke. They are not a tight, disciplined criminal organisation;
they are a loose, overlapping, often squabbling crew who happen to be very good at staying
unfindable, and who treat every near-miss with the Cyber Police as content.

- **The Commodore.** The figurehead, never confirmed by a real name, who narrates the site's
  banner messages and, occasionally, taunts the unit directly in them. Nobody on the floor is
  certain the Commodore is one person.
- **The Quartermasters.** The moderator crew, who keep the index running and the uploads
  "seaworthy." Several have been identified, none successfully charged; the ones who are caught
  tend to be the least important.
- **The Bosun.** Runs the infrastructure: the mirrors, the proxy chains, the "scuttle protocol"
  that lets the whole site go dark and resurface somewhere else within hours of a takedown. The
  Bosun is the unit's real target and the one lead that has never once held up.

## Why the unit can never quite land it

Every time the Cyber Police gets close — a server located, a proxy chain traced, a warrant signed
— something happens. A mirror comes up somewhere else before the raid team is through the door.
A seized drive turns out to hold nothing but a looping video of a rubber duck. A tip comes in that
sends half the unit to the wrong warehouse while the real one empties overnight. The floor calls
these **Scuttle luck**, and argues, at length and without resolution, over how much of it is
genuine skill, inside information, or the unit simply being underfunded and outgunned by a crew
that treats getting caught as a story to tell.

## The standing joke

The Scuttle's operators know who the Cyber Police are, know several of them by name, and have
twice left messages addressed to the unit directly inside a seized server's root directory. The
most recent one read: "Nice try. Bring snacks next time." The Quartermaster has not stopped
bringing it up.
