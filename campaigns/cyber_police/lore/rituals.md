# Rituals and Working Hours

## The hours

The Cyber Police keep standard department hours: **07:00 to 19:00**, five days a week on paper,
six in practice, because The Scuttle doesn't observe a schedule and leads don't wait for Monday.
Unlike Ashiorid's round-the-clock floor, the unit does go home — but everyone on it has, at some
point, been paged back in at an hour that made the rest of their week harder.

## 07:00: The briefing

The unit gathers around the shared monitor bank. The Captain gives **the directive**: the lead
the unit is chasing today, drawn from whatever changed on The Scuttle overnight — a new mirror,
a chatter spike, a tip from the one friendly reporter. Each role then says in a line what they're
picking up:

- The Analyst names what the intelligence says.
- The Detective names the plan, or says when it will exist.
- The Agent and Forensics say what they're picking up technically and on evidence.
- Press says what, if anything, needs managing today.
- The Quartermaster gives supply notices: what's been requisitioned, what's been denied, what
  needs signing out before anyone leaves the building.

The Observer attends every briefing, stands by the stairwell door and says nothing. The Captain
starts talking once the Observer is in the room — a habit nobody has named out loud but everyone
has noticed.

## The working day

After the briefing the Analyst works the intelligence picture and the Detective turns it into an
investigation plan. By midday the Agent and Forensics should each have a task with their name on
it. If they don't, the Detective knows it and so does everyone else.

## Midday

Lunch happens whenever there's a gap, usually at the desks, because the basement has no good
coffee anywhere within a ten-minute walk upstairs and nobody trusts the vending machine's
sandwiches. Two customs apply:

- Nobody brings up "Scuttle luck" at lunch. It always starts an argument.
- Press is allowed to ask the Agent "what did we actually find" without being told it's
  classified.

## The evening

From mid-afternoon the unit works toward whatever the day's lead resolves to: an arrest, a dead
end, or — most often — a lead that goes quiet and gets logged for next time. Forensics closes out
the evidence log, the Detective signs off the day's reports, and the Captain closes the case file
if it's closed, or leaves it open with a note for the morning.

## Requisition day

Once a week the Quartermaster submits the unit's equipment requests upstairs. Most are denied or
delayed; she logs every denial in a binder she calls, without much humour, "the eventual inquiry."
When something is finally approved, it's usually a generation behind what it needed to be.

## The end of day

The Quartermaster is first in and last to lock the supply cage. Whoever's still at a desk past
19:00 gets asked, politely, whether it's worth the overtime paperwork. The Observer is never seen
leaving. Nobody has mentioned this to Internal Affairs, because nobody is sure what they'd say.

## Other customs

- **Scuttle luck.** Any theory about how The Scuttle keeps slipping a raid gets argued over for at
  least ten minutes before anyone lets it go.
- **The duck video.** The rubber-duck loop found on a seized drive two years ago is still the
  unit's background wallpaper on one monitor, as a reminder.
