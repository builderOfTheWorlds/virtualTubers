# The Cyber Police

The Cyber Police are a seven-person digital-crimes unit attached to a metropolitan police
department that does not, in its own budget documents, know quite what to call them. The unit's
official name, stencilled on a door at the end of a basement corridor, is the **Digital Crimes
Task Force**. Nobody on the floor uses it. They call themselves what the tabloids call them,
because the tabloids got there first: **the Cyber Police**.

## Mandate

The unit exists to investigate, disrupt and prosecute organised digital crime: piracy rings,
extortion crews, botnets, and the loose, overlapping world of people who call themselves hackers
whether or not they can write code. Their single standing case, open longer than anyone currently
on the floor has been assigned to it, is **The Scuttle** — the piracy site described in
`the_scuttle.md` — and the crew that runs it.

## Founding

The unit was stood up eleven years ago, after a budget line appeared with no public announcement
and a lot of private argument. The department needed somewhere to put "the computer stuff" and
did not want to put it anywhere expensive. The basement corridor was free. The name Digital Crimes
Task Force was chosen by a committee and never loved by anyone who had to say it out loud.

## What nobody takes seriously

This is the fact the unit lives inside, the same way Ashiorid's floor lives inside the Tallow
Street hours. Every value on the laminated card by their door exists because somebody, somewhere,
once waved the Cyber Police off as a joke:

1. **It isn't a real crime until it's a real door.** Uniformed officers get backup dispatched in
   under four minutes. The Cyber Police's last three requests for backup were logged, acknowledged,
   and never sent.
2. **Nobody asks what we caught.** The unit's conviction rate is better than two of the department's
   violent-crime units. It is never in the department's year-end report.
3. **The badge still works on the door.** Whatever anyone upstairs calls them, the badge opens
   the evidence locker and the warrant goes through the same judge as everyone else's.
4. **We put our lives on the line the same as anyone with a gun on their hip.** Said without
   irony, by every member of the unit, usually right before somebody upstairs laughs.

## Funding and equipment

The unit runs on requisitions, not a standing budget — see `rituals.md`. Every laptop, every
license, every evidence drive is logged, argued for, and usually granted six weeks late and one
generation of hardware behind what The Scuttle's crew is running. The Quartermaster has strong
feelings about this and a drawer full of denied requisition forms kept, she says, "for the
eventual inquiry."

## Public image

The press call them, depending on the week, "the department's IT department with badges," or
worse. The unit's one friendly press contact, cultivated carefully over years by the Press
Liaison, is the only reason the public has ever heard the unit's name attached to a real case
instead of a punchline. See `respect.md`.
