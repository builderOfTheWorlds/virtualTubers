# The Amulet of Wonder

Nine monuments are scattered across the continent, each one a sealed
doorway into a dungeon, each dungeon guarding a fragment of a map. Jasper and
Juniper hold the blank map that fills itself in one fragment at a time, and
they only ever know the location of the next unopened door — never the whole
route. Solve a monument's riddle aloud, in the language it was carved in, and
its door opens; the map draws the next mark on its own.

When the ninth fragment lands, the map marks a burial site: the Amulet of
Wonder itself, solid gold, one great ruby at its center and six lesser rubies
around the rim.

The amulet does not come off once worn. Six trials light its six lesser
rubies one at a time — taking a life, falling to nothing, sacrificing
something that mattered, stealing greatly, gaining great wealth, losing
everything — and only once all six are lit can its wearer say the words and
make a wish. The stakes are real: say the wish and fail the roll, and the
amulet disintegrates along with the chance.

Nobody in living memory has lit all six rubies. The nine dungeons are old
enough, and dangerous enough, that most who go looking for the amulet never
finish counting the doors.
