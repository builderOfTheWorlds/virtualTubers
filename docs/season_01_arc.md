## 1. THE SEASON ANCHOR (lock this before airing loop 1)

Everything else in this doc is negotiable. This is not — the seeds in Loop 1
must point somewhere real, or the mystery-box failure mode (Lost, Westworld)
is guaranteed.

**Proposal — the three-layer answer:**

**Layer 1 (what the cast eventually learns):** They are in a loop. Their
father's murder repeats. They have done this hundreds of times.

**Layer 2 (what the audience learns at the macro midpoint, Loop 4):**
The antagonist isn't causing the loop. **He's trapped in it too** — and has
been trying to break out for ten thousand years. Every "attempt to destroy
the world" the party has heroically stopped was an escape attempt. **The
party are the reason the loop persists.** They are the immune system. They
have always been the immune system. Their father built them for it.

**Layer 3 (the finale, Loop 7):** The Event was the loop starting. Someone
initiated it deliberately — and the strongest candidate is **Duke Leto
III**, who dies in the first hour of every loop precisely because a man who
dies early is a man nobody investigates. The mana worm isn't a weapon or a
resource. **It's the mechanism.** Protecting it is what keeps the loop
running. The party's entire heroic arc, every loop, is the thing that
imprisons them.

**Loop 7's actual question**: knowing all of it, do they protect the worm
one more time — or let Muad'Malik have it and find out what's outside?

**Why this anchor works:**
- It reframes canon rather than contradicting it. Every established fact
  (Leto's breeding program, the scattered children, the vault, "protect
  this") stays literally true and flips in meaning.
- The Loop 1 seeds already point at it — Grovley's *"it's been quite some
  time"*, the compulsion in the note, the party remembering nothing before
  age six.
- The antagonist becomes tragic without being redeemed. He's still a monster;
  he's just a monster who was right.
- It gives the sage line a purpose: the sage is **the loop's leak**, the one
  variable the system can't fully wipe.

⚠️ **Decision required**: is Leto the architect (proposed) or another
victim? The whole season points differently depending. Recommendation: Leto,
because it makes the show's most sympathetic canon moment — a father saving
his children — the exact mechanism of their imprisonment.

## 2. Loop-by-loop sketches

Each loop is a **complete standalone story** (9 beats, Mon–Sun) whose
**Friday midpoint** carries one macro turn.

### LOOP 1 — "The Anniversary" *(fully scripted)*
**Genre**: high fantasy. **Macro position**: normalcy + first glitch.
**Sage**: The Old Lady (final loop of her tenure) → hands off to a PC.
See [`loop_01_hour_by_hour.md`](loop_01_hour_by_hour.md).

### LOOP 2 — "The One Who Remembers"
**Macro turn**: *first real evidence of repetition.*

The new sage (a PC from Loop 1) wakes with **everything**. The show's POV
inverts: for the first time we follow someone who knows.

- **The convincing problem** *(the great time-loop scene)*: they have six
  days to make three strangers — their own siblings — believe them. They
  fail, mostly.
- **Foreknowledge as spectacle**: they know the guards' line, know Grovley's
  greeting, know the attack comes Tuesday at 18:00. They try to save Leto.
  **They cannot.** Something always intervenes.
- **The cost**: by Saturday they're being treated as unwell. The party
  restrains them.
- **Friday midpoint**: they prove it — predicting something impossible in
  advance, on stream. The others believe for about four hours. Then the loop
  produces a mundane explanation and the belief dissolves.
- **Reset**: their tenure has one loop left.

*Production note*: this is the strongest single-loop premise in the season
and probably the best episode. Consider airing it second precisely because
it hooks anyone who found Loop 1 slow.

### LOOP 3 — "Ten Thousand Doors" *(GENRE HOP)*
**Macro turn**: *consequences compound; the frame widens.*

The reset lands somewhere else entirely — **cyberpunk**. Same souls,
different simulation layer. A hacker crew, a corporate arcology, a data
heist. The Duke is a CEO. Grovley is a building AI. The worm is a process
nobody can kill.

- The sage (2nd and final loop of tenure) recognizes people across the genre
  boundary. Nobody else does.
- **The audience gets the reveal the cast doesn't**: this isn't one world
  looping. It's *many*, and the same story keeps happening in all of them.
- Proves the platform's genre-agnostic engine on air, as a story beat rather
  than a tech demo.
- **Friday midpoint**: the sage finds, in the arcology's oldest archive, a
  file describing the events of Loop 1 — as history.
- **Reset**: sage tenure ends. New sage anointed. First full handoff the
  audience has seen *coming*.

### LOOP 4 — "The Immune System" — **MACRO MIDPOINT**
**Macro turn**: *Layer 2 lands. The show changes.*

Back to Ashiorid. This is the season's hinge.

- Muad'Malik, cornered at the climax, finally gets a full scene instead of a
  death. He explains — coherently, exhaustedly, with evidence — that he is
  not trying to destroy anything.
- **He shows them the count.** How many times. The number is obscene.
- **The party realizes they are the antagonists** of his story, and always
  have been.
- **Friday midpoint** is the pivot: they discover the previous sages'
  records, and every single one reached this same conclusion, and every
  single one was wiped before acting on it.
- **Sunday**: for the first time, they hesitate at the climax. They win
  anyway — the loop makes sure of it. But it's the first loop that ends
  with the audience unsure who to root for.

### LOOP 5 — "Complicity"
**Macro turn**: *fallout reframes everything prior.*

- The party tries to **cooperate** with Muad'Malik. It goes badly — ten
  thousand years has left him genuinely unfit to be an ally. Canon's madness
  pays off here as a *plot obstacle*, not a character note.
- **Rewatch bomb**: they revisit Loop 1's events and the audience sees every
  earlier scene reinterpreted. Consider literally replaying Loop 1 footage
  in dead hours this week (the platform already does replays).
- **Friday midpoint**: the sage line's true function surfaces — the sage
  isn't a gift. It's a **leak the system tolerates** because a little
  knowledge keeps the party motivated enough to keep winning.
- **Darkest hour**: they deliberately lose. The loop resets early. **The
  first broken rule of the format** — and the only time it should ever
  happen. (Note: this contradicts the "loops always run full-length" rule on
  purpose, which is exactly why it lands.)

### LOOP 6 — "The Long Week"
**Macro turn**: *darkest hour at season scale.*

- The loop **fights back**. Reality gets thin: NPCs repeat lines, geography
  loops, the terminal glitches *during* the week rather than only at reset.
- The sage handoff **fails** — nobody is at the trigger point. A loop with
  no sage. Total amnesia. The audience watches the cast start from absolute
  zero, knowing everything they've lost.
- **This is the season's cruelest hour**: the audience has all the
  knowledge and the cast has none, and there is no bridge.
- **Friday midpoint**: Grovley. He's been doing this longer than anyone.
  **He was the first sage, and he was skipped** — left conscious, wiped of
  authority but not memory, condemned to greet the same four strangers
  forever. *(See ambient bank §12.)*
- **Reset**: Grovley chooses to become the sage again, ending his own
  neutrality.

### LOOP 7 — "The Ten Thousandth" — **SEASON FINALE**
**Macro turn**: *the climax. Layer 3 lands.*

- The invitation's canon wording finally resolves: this really is the **ten
  thousandth** anniversary. The "1,000 vs 10,000" source inconsistency
  becomes a plot point instead of an error — the number was never wrong, the
  *calendar* was reset.
- **The Leto reveal** (Layer 3): he built it, he dies first every loop by
  design, the worm is the mechanism.
- **The choice**: protect the worm one more time, or hand it to Muad'Malik.
- **Do not resolve cleanly.** End the season on the reset firing *mid-choice*
  — the audience doesn't learn which they picked until Season 2, loop 1,
  when the world either looks the same or doesn't.

## 3. Sage tenure ledger

Tenure = 2 loops, staggered so a handoff happens every loop.

| Loop | Sage | Tenure | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Old Lady | year 2 of 2 | Dies Sat; knowledge passes at reset |
| 2 | PC (from L1) | year 1 of 2 | The convincing problem |
| 3 | PC (same) | year 2 of 2 | Cross-genre recognition |
| 4 | New PC | year 1 of 2 | Inherits at the macro midpoint |
| 5 | Same PC | year 2 of 2 | Discovers the sage line's real function |
| 6 | **NONE** | — | Handoff fails. The cruelest loop. |
| 7 | Grovley | year 1 | The first sage, returning |

**The Loop 6 gap is the ledger's whole point** — establish the rhythm for
five loops so breaking it in the sixth actually costs something.

## 4. Escalation curves (what changes week to week)

Keep every one of these monotonic. The audience should be able to *feel* the
season progressing even in ambient hours.

| Axis | L1 | L4 | L7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Glitch frequency** | reset only | occasional mid-week | constant |
| **Dream fragments/week** | 3 | 6 | 12+, while awake |
| **Cast loop-awareness** | zero | partial, resisted | total |
| **Muad'Malik screen time** | 1 hr, unnamed | 5 hrs, named, coherent | co-protagonist |
| **Grovley's strangeness** | one line | several | he addresses the audience |
| **Terminal integrity** | clean | artifacts | pane bleed, wrong fonts |
| **Recap reliability** | accurate | small errors | **actively lying** |

**The recap curve is the sleeper**: a recap that quietly misremembers is the
cheapest, most unsettling escalation available, and it exploits the one
format element every viewer trusts.

## 5. Genre-hop slate (loops 3+)

The engine is genre-agnostic; the reset needs no bridge. Candidates:

| Genre | The Duke | The worm | The sage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cyberpunk** *(L3)* | Arcology CEO | An unkillable process | Building AI |
| **Age of sail** | A merchant prince | Cargo nobody may open | The ship's navigator |
| **Mining colony / hard SF** | Colony administrator | The core sample | Comms officer |
| **Weird west** | Railroad baron | A sealed strongbox | The undertaker |
| **Modern espionage** | An oligarch | A briefcase | A handler |
| **Post-apocalypse** | Vault warden | The seed bank | The archivist |

**Rule**: the *roles* persist across genres, the *flavor* doesn't. That's
the whole thesis of the platform, and Loop 3 should state it visually — the
same ASCII face, different costume.

## 6. Open

1. **Leto as architect** — lock or reject (§1). Blocks everything.
2. **Which PC becomes the Loop 2 sage** — decided by Loop 1's Sunday 22:00
   staging, per the happenstance recommendation.
3. **Genre-hop timing** — is Loop 3 too early to break the fantasy setting?
   Counter-argument: it's exactly early enough that the audience learns the
   show can do anything before they've settled.
4. **Season 2** — does the loop break, or does the show admit it's a
   perpetual format? This determines whether Loop 7 is an ending or a hinge.
