## Established canon (usable near-verbatim)

The "duke's hidden children, gathered via invitation" premise we designed
independently already exists as real, load-bearing canon — confirmed
across three separate files, not a one-off:

- **Duke Sorensen Leto III** secretly fathered the PCs through a breeding
  program run by the Begene Sisters; he rebelled, hid the children in
  separate corners of the world to protect them.
- **The invitation** (`Invitation.md`, verbatim): *"The Duke Sorensen Leto
  / Requests the pleasure / Of your company at / The Ten Thousandth
  Anniversary of / The Event / R.S.V.P. to @Ashiorid"* — each PC receives
  it independently, compelled to attend.
- Guards mutter **"one of them"** as the PCs arrive; the butler
  **Grovley** greets each by name as though they've met before.
- Mid-party, an unnamed BBEG and his army attack; his wizard casts a mass
  hold spell, he kills Leto personally, then burns the castle.
- Grovley reveals the truth in a hidden subbasement — Leto was their
  father — and hands off a broken signet ring plus a stolen magical
  creature (a "mana worm") the party must smuggle out and protect.
- Independently corroborated from a different PC's POV in `Helen.md`
  (real name Alcinoe): same lineage, same rescue, same breeding program.

## Blank canvas (referenced but never defined — free to claim)

- **"The Event"** — the cataclysm 1,000 years back that Ashiorid's entire
  calendar is measured from. `Campaign Timeline.md` lists its nature as an
  open "Mystery Element," deliberately left undefined by the original DM.
- **"The Watcher"** — referenced exactly once, as an unbuilt side-quest
  stub in `Campaign Index.md`. No character, backstory, or lore attached.

Per `PROJECT INSTRUCTIONS.md` (the vault's own import-process doc), both
empty files are near-certainly Obsidian auto-stubs created because other
notes link to them, not deliberately-emptied content — the import is
marked 100% complete, so nothing is "missing" here to go dig up.

## Strong adaptation candidates (real, but need reinterpretation)

- **Sultan Muad'Malik** — leader of the Bahadur, an immortal faction
  trapped in Ashiorid since The Event. Took a head injury at The Event
  that "over ten thousand years... evolved into a serious mental illness";
  now cult-like, unraveling, dabbles in necromancy. Closest existing match
  to a "static antagonist warped by millennia of accumulated
  time" — **but canon attributes the madness to a physical wound
  degenerating over ordinary elapsed time, not to memory itself.**
  Reframing it as loop-memory-driven is a reinterpretation, not a direct
  read of the source. Plausibly (not confirmed in canon) the unnamed BBEG
  who kills Leto.
- **The unnamed "Old Lady"** — ancient, hooded, tasked with "educating"
  the party about The Event in the opening scene. Closest sage-analog, but
  a single-scene stub with no established immortality or memory mechanic.
- **Bahadur Leadership** — became immortal when trapped by The Event;
  spent ten thousand years breeding creatures/humans trying to engineer a
  way home. Supports a "beings outside normal time" flavor.
- **Drokki RedGem Finnsson** — becomes immortal, later forgotten by the
  world while still alive. Adjacent "immortality erodes identity" theme,
  not a sage mechanic.

## Precedent, but not equivalent to a loop

- `Age of War.md` (an earlier/alternate draft later reworked into the
  Leto/Grovley version above): both branch endings resolve to "in the far
  future, the new players will have to fight this battle again" — a
  generational-succession theme, but linear (no one remembers the prior
  iteration), not a repeating reset of a short time window.
- `Campaign Timeline.md` flags its own suspicion of "cyclical extreme
  weather every ~300 years" as an open, unconfirmed question — not
  established lore.

## ⚠️ CANON RELIABILITY WARNING (added 2026-08-02, second pass)

**The vault was machine-imported on 2025-12-03 and the importer expanded
thin source files into long, polished, authoritative-looking documents.**
Much of what reads as canon is generated scaffolding.

**How to check**: every file has an italic `*Source:*` footer. Files citing a
real source `.txt` read as authentic. Sections headed *"Suggested"*,
*"To Develop"*, *"Roleplay Suggestions"*, *"DM Notes"*, or *"Mysteries"* are
import-time invention.

**Proof case — Grovley.** `NPCs/Grovley.md` is 8.8 KB. Line 321 of that file
admits, verbatim: *"Note: Source file contains only the word 'Butler' - this
content is expanded from context in The Party Attack scene"*. **The DM's
original Grovley file was one word.**

Real Grovley canon is only what appears in the Plots files: he is Leto III's
butler, he tells the PCs they are siblings from the Begene Sisters'
experiment, he reveals they hold an object preventing dimensional invasions,
and he helps them escape. Everything else — his appearance, personality,
"he KNOWS things he shouldn't", "I have been expecting you" — is generated.

**Verified-authentic files** (cite real sources): Azra, Ylva, Drokki.
**Verified-generated**: Grovley.

### High-value finds

**YLVA** — `NPCs/Ylva.md`, source-verified. **The single best adaptation
target found so far.**

- Human, **14 years old**, trapped in the Frozen Fortress; both parents
  presumed dead. Survived alone.
- Eyes: *"bright green that almost radiates light."*
- Power: **foresight, a few seconds into the future.** Canon even scripts
  her voice: *"Wait! Something's about to—"*
- *"Innocent but haunted."* *"Her foresight sometimes shows her terrible
  futures."*
- **The canon hook that matters most**, quoted: *"If the party successfully
  gets Ylva to safety, her lineage will survive to the far future. A very
  small amount of people will carry her foresight, and it will be a selected
  genetic trait by the breeding program."*

**Implication**: the setting contains a **program that manufactures
prophecy**, still running. The prophet here isn't a person, it's a pipeline —
and Ylva is upstream of the PCs' own breeding program.

**AZRA** — `NPCs/Azra.md`, source-verified, densest NPC file (9 KB).
Bahadur assassin from Ord, *"one of the first through the portal."*

- Skin *"almost pure white — artificially lightened"* for disguise; eyes
  *"glowing faint blue."* **She doesn't know why** her caste has blue eyes —
  *"just accepts it as fact."*
- Fatalistic: claims she is *"already a dead woman."* Carries poison sewn
  into her suit; must be physically stopped from suicide.
- **Two deliberately-open mysteries**: surgical scars on abdomen and back
  (*"Leave this ambiguous"*), and **a metal flower, slightly lavender, that
  smells like lilac** — significance unstated.
- **Shares no language with the party.** Magical translation required.
- Canon states the Bahadur motive plainly: Ord is *"a dying world that has
  run out of magic"*; magic is *"a resource (finite, exhaustible)."*
- Names the Bahadur leadership: Dema, Batul, Tayyib, Zaki, **Sultan
  Muad'Malik** (a Paladin).

**DROKKI** — source-verified. Immortal dwarf smith, and the "forgotten"
element is stronger than previously reported:

- **Immortality mechanism is literally just "survived the Frozen Wastes."**
  No ritual, no artifact, unexplained by design.
- Grows restless after *"a long time of not dying"*, leaves, goes missing.
- **Vabokedos erects a monument to him — while he is still alive**, in a
  cell under Uksossnong. The statue outlasts the memory of the man.
- His **sentient greataxe** talks, is indestructible, teleports to hand —
  and **prevents short rests within 300 feet.** An immortal with a weapon
  that forbids rest is nearly too on-the-nose for this show.

**THE ROB BOSS** — a Bob Ross parody, written as a full scene. Windowless
brick building, single blue door, hallway of colored doors, white door with
two armed guards. Inside: a very old man with *"strangely spherical hair"*,
a *"small woodland creature"* chirping on his shoulder. He ignores the party
for minutes, then: *"I think this one is finished."* Turns. **"Hello, I'm The
Rob Boss, and I'd like you to steal a painting for me."** Self-contained,
zero plot dependency — ideal drop-in segment.

**LEENA** — tonally alien to the rest of the vault (guns, "the law", an
X-Men-style powered-kid chase). Reads as imported from a different setting.
Personality, in full: *"Likes to climb trees."*

### Political layer

## Contradictions / things to resolve if adapting

- **No literal time loop or reset mechanic exists anywhere in the
  source.** This stays original invention layered on top of the setting.
- **Sage rotation (2-loop tenure, handoff between two characters) has no
  canon basis at all.**
- Muad'Malik's madness: physical injury (canon) vs. weight-of-memory
  (our design) — pick one framing if he's adopted as the antagonist.
- Source-internal inconsistency: `Invitation.md` says "Ten Thousandth
  Anniversary," everything else (`Campaign Opening`, `Campaign Timeline`)
  implies 1,000 years. Not our error — pick one if reusing the invitation
  text verbatim.
