# Recording Your Voice — A Guide for Our Aussie Mate

G'day! Thanks heaps for helping. We're making a computer voice that sounds like
you, for the characters in our streaming show. All you need to do is **read some
sentences out loud** while recording. We handle all the techy stuff on our end.

There are only two stages:

1. **Test run (about 15 minutes):** read 50 sentences and send them over.
   We check the sound and tell you whether anything needs adjusting.
2. **The real thing (about 6–8 short sessions over a couple of weeks):** read
   more sentences, around 30–40 minutes per session.

Most of the effort is the first setup. After that it's just pressing record and reading.

---

## Before you start: say yes properly

The voice will be used for characters in our live Twitch streams. We'll send
you a short consent note to read and reply "agreed" to. It says what we can and
can't do with your voice. You can change your mind at any time, and we'll
delete the voice and all your recordings.

---

## Step 1 — Pick how you'll record

Choose ONE of these. Option A gives the best result.

**Option A — Computer + USB microphone (best)**
- A USB microphone is around $80–$150 at JB Hi-Fi or similar (e.g. Rode
  NT-USB Mini, Rode PodMic USB, or a Fifine). A gaming headset mic also works.
- Avoid the laptop's built-in mic: it picks up fan noise and room echo.
- Install **Audacity**, a free recording program: go to **audacityteam.org**,
  click Download, and install it like any other program.

**Option B — iPhone (fine for a start)**
- Use the built-in **Voice Memos** app.
- Before recording, turn on high quality: open **Settings → Apps → Voice
  Memos → Audio Quality** and choose **Lossless**.
- Android phones vary a lot, so send us your phone model first and we'll
  tell you what to use.

---

## Step 2 — Set up your spot

Your recording space matters more than the mic. Find:

- **A quiet room with soft stuff in it:** a bedroom with a bed, curtains and
  carpet is perfect. Avoid kitchens and bathrooms (too echoey).
- **Turn off** fans, air-con, the telly, and anything that hums. Close the windows.
- **Put your phone on silent** (or flight mode, if it isn't the recorder).
- **Sit down** and place the mic about **one hand-span (about 20 cm)** from
  your mouth, slightly off to the side so your breath doesn't hit it directly.
- Have a **glass of water** nearby.

**Use the same room, mic and seating position every session.** This is the
most important rule. If things change between sessions, the voice comes out patchy.

---

## Step 3 — Do a quick sound check (Option A only)

1. Open Audacity and plug in the mic.
2. Near the top there's a microphone dropdown. Pick your USB mic.
3. Next to it, set the channels to **1 (Mono)**.
4. Set the sample rate to **44100**: open **Audio Setup → Audio Settings**
   and find "Project Sample Rate". Older versions have a "Project Rate" box in
   the bottom-left corner instead. If you can't find it, don't stress; the
   default is fine.
5. Click the red **Record** button and say "G'day, testing one two three" at
   normal volume. Press the **Stop** square.
6. Look at the wiggly blue line. The tallest bits should reach about
   **halfway to three-quarters** of the box height.
   - Barely visible? Move the mic a bit closer.
   - Hitting the top and bottom edges? Move back a bit, or turn the input
     volume (the slider with the mic icon) down.
7. Delete the test: Edit → Select All, then press Delete.

---

## Step 4 — How to read the sentences

You'll get a numbered list of sentences (the first one is `pilot_50.txt`).
Have it on a screen or printed out in front of you.

**For each sentence:**
1. **Say the number**, e.g. "Number one."
2. **Pause** for about 1 second.
3. **Read the sentence.**
4. **Pause** for about 2 seconds before the next number.

So it sounds like this:

> "Number one." *(pause)* "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog." *(pause, pause)*
> "Number two." *(pause)* "G'day mate, how's it going?" *(pause, pause)*

We use the numbers and pauses to cut the recording into separate sentences
automatically, so you don't need one file per sentence.

**Tips for sounding good:**
- **Talk like you're telling a mate a story.** Don't do a "radio voice" or a
  posh voice. We want YOUR normal accent and how you really talk.
- **Questions should sound like questions, and exclamations should have some
  energy.** Some lines come from fantasy adventure stories, so feel free to
  give them a bit of drama.
- **Keep the same distance** from the mic the whole time.
- **Made a mistake? Don't stop the recording.** Pause, say "**again**",
  then say the number and read the sentence again. We'll keep the last good
  attempt.
- If a word is unclear or you'd never say it, read it anyway the way you
  naturally would.
- **Take breaks.** Every 50 or so sentences, pause the recording and have a
  sip of water. If your voice gets tired or croaky, stop for the day. A
  tired voice sounds different, and we want it consistent.
- Avoid recording right after waking up, or while you have a cold.

---

## Step 5 — Save the recording

**Option A (Audacity):**
1. Press Stop when finished.
2. Go to **File → Export Audio** (older versions: File → Export → Export as WAV).
3. Choose **WAV** as the format (Signed 16-bit PCM).
4. Name it like this: **`pilot_yourname.wav`**. For later sessions use
   `batch01_yourname.wav`, `batch02_yourname.wav`, etc.
5. Save it to your Desktop.

**Option B (iPhone):**
1. Tap the recording, then tap the three dots **(…)** → **Rename**, and use
   the same naming as above.

---

## Step 6 — Send it to us

- Upload the file to the **shared Google Drive folder link** we've sent you
  (open the link, then tap or click **New → File upload**).
  - On iPhone, from Voice Memos: tap **(…) → Share → Save to Files** or
    **Drive**.
- Send us a quick message saying "batch uploaded". Mention anything odd, like a
  dog barking halfway through or skipped lines.

That's it! For the test run, we'll reply within a day or two to say whether it
sounds good or what to tweak (usually "move closer" or "find a quieter
room"). After that we'll send the next list of sentences.
